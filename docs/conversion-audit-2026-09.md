## Model pass

The offer is sound and the page does not sell it. The one structural problem on the site is the stack of a .clinic domain, an unlicensed coach, and a page whose spine is recovery sold as the outcome: 'Really Good Recovery' in the title tag and H1, 'Doctor of Physical Therapy candidate' in the credential strip, six named testimonials and twelve labelled faces with no consent on file under a 'Real recovery' headline, page-authored diagnosis headlines, and no scope sentence in either footer. The copy leg of that stack is the whole frame of the page, so it is a repositioning from recovery to capacity, not a word sweep; the domain leg is a residual you accepted on 2026-08-09 and have not had a lawyer check. Everything else is copy. The price is hidden on the page and sprung in a Calendly form whose $500-$700 band does not cover the $850 tier. The page speaks to everyday athletes, weekend warriors, and competitors, never says 'business', and does not say BJJ until the About section. The refuters killed the case for cutting Remote, for repricing to a weekly rung, for dropping 'Program' from the name, for an hourly-rate anchor, and for cutting the high-level competitor from the reader. One thing to confirm before any session count ships: the brief says Hybrid $685 is weekly in-person; your own Stripe note describes it as one in-person session a month. Both cannot go on the page.

### The offer: holds
The offer fits the buyer and nothing in it needs to change: a weekly in-person session, programming Chase builds and tunes, a quarterly in-person retest, Chase owning every log, a direct business line, and bonuses that speak to a man juggling business travel and competition. Remote stays as a public rung: the brief defines it as part of the offer, scope-guardrails clears it on the condition that it sells capacity and takes a training intake, and the bar's entire conversion path is a priced remote rung, so the case for cutting it does not hold. The problem is that the live page sells a different product. Packages shows three unnamed, unpriced lanes, one of which ('The Program', 'You run it on your own') is not something you sell, the two in-person cadences you actually bill are collapsed into one card, and the note under them says every package starts with the same assessment. That is a Packages rewrite, not an offer change. Two housekeeping items: the quarterly retest is listed as both a core deliverable and a bonus, and it can only be one; the video demo library stays off the page until phase two ships (the live page does not mention it today, so nothing to cut). One thing must be settled before a session count goes on the page: the brief says Hybrid $685 is weekly in-person, and your own Stripe catalog note describes Hybrid as one in-person session a month and Hybrid 2x as two a month. The refuters ruled that note outside this audit's evidence, which is why it is not structural here, but it is your note, and 'weekly' cannot ship until Stripe says the same thing.

### The price: holds
Survivable if the three monthly numbers sit on the page next to what each one buys. Not survivable as it is: the page shows no dollar figure anywhere, and the only CTA opens a Calendly form that springs '$500-$700/month with a minimum 3-month commitment' on a stranger, behind a cookie banner, with a required Select, and that band does not cover the $850 tier. The '$165 an hour, same rate on every rung' anchor comes from outside the brief, and the brief's own prices do not produce it: the rungs step by $170 and $165 a month, which is the price of one added weekly session, not an hourly rate, and a cerebral reader will do that arithmetic next to '$685 a month'. 'What a good Boca coach already costs him' is a comparison the brief never establishes. What the evidence supports: print $515, $685, and $850 with the deliverables under each, carry the founding-rate line (these are founding rates; the first cohort goes before the numbers exist and their retests become the proof), and give Remote at $515 a reason to cost twice the bar's public $250 remote rung: founder-built 1:1, no app, no community feed, Chase owns the log. No hourly equivalent, no competitor-coach comparison, and no 3-month minimum on the page until you confirm that term is a product you actually sell.

### The program name: holds

'Program' is green and is the category's own noun for coaching, not just templates: the bar's title is 'The Official Strength & Conditioning Program', its champion quote says 'designed a special program that fits my game', and its coaching service sits under a Programming nav, so the case for dropping the word does not hold. ### Title tag and meta
**Why the current version leaks.** Four leaks, in order of consequence. (1) The home title tag is the copy leg of the .clinic stack at its shortest: a .clinic domain, an unlicensed coach, and a browser tab that reads 'HMN AIDE — Really Good Recovery.' A search result, a shared link, and a regulator's first glance all read that line before any page copy, and 'recovery' sells a clinical outcome as the brand's promise. It names no sport, no buyer, and no place; the bar's title tag names BJJ in its first line. (2) Neither page has a meta description, so Google writes the snippet itself, and the most likely source is the hero sub: 'Aches, injuries, and slow recovery don't fix themselves with rest.' That puts 'fix' (red list) and injury language (headline-banned) into the search result, outside anyone's control. (3) Neither page has og:title, og:description, og:image, or a twitter card. The brief's traffic sources are Instagram, DMs, referrals, and Substack, which means most first visits arrive as a pasted link, and a pasted hmnaide.clinic link unfurls with nothing but the domain. That is a business card with only the domain printed on it, and the domain says clinic. (4) Both titles carry an em dash, which the voice file bans, and 'Contact — HMN AIDE' mislabels the page: a gym owner searching for a mobility workshop cannot find it, and a coaching prospect who lands on 'Contact' finds a form that asks about his event.

**What it fails to answer.** External: the reader's very first contact with the business is a search result, a tab, or a link preview, and today none of those tells the business owner who trains BJJ in South Florida that this page is for him. The niche verdict found 'business' nowhere on the page and BJJ first appearing in About; the title and meta are where that gap starts, one screen before the hero. Internal: decision fatigue and the delegation promise (Chase builds it, logs it, retests it; the client shows up) are the reason he would click, and nothing in the metadata says it. Peripheral: the scope authority names the combination of a .clinic domain, recovery-and-pain copy, and an unlicensed operator as the holding-out exposure, and the title tag is the shortest, most visible instance of that combination on the site.

**What it costs.** Every search impression and every shared link is the page's first CTA, and right now it makes a clinical promise to nobody in particular. Off-niche readers (the everyday athletes and weekend warriors the old copy invited) click, do not find themselves, and either bounce or fill the Calendly form and fail the money qualifier, which costs Chase call time. On-niche readers get no signal that a founder-built, in-person, BJJ-specific option exists in Boca Raton, while the bar's title gives them its signal in the same search. Links pasted into DMs and IG stories, the channels that actually carry traffic, unfurl as a bare .clinic domain with no title, description, or image. And the tab title is the one line an attorney or a regulator reads before anything else, and it currently says the wrong thing. No conversion or search numbers exist for the site (current conversion: unknown), so no figure is claimed.
